## What Is a Mutual Fund Screener?

A mutual fund screener is a research tool that lets users filter funds using selected criteria. Depending on the platform, these criteria may include fund category, risk measures, expense ratio, historical performance, or other published data.

A screener helps narrow a large list of funds. It does not automatically identify the best fund for every investor.

Before relying on a screener, check the data date, definitions, filters, and source of the information.

## What Is Mutual Fund Comparison?

A mutual fund comparison examines two or more funds side by side.

Investors may compare fund objectives, categories, risk measures, expense ratios, benchmark information, portfolio holdings, and historical performance.

Comparisons should use consistent periods and relevant categories. Comparing the historical return of two funds over different periods can lead to misleading conclusions.

Past performance also does not guarantee future performance.

## What Is Mutual Fund Overlap?

Mutual fund overlap refers to securities that appear in more than one fund's portfolio. For example, two equity funds may hold some of the same companies.

Some overlap can be expected, especially when funds invest in similar market segments. A large amount of overlap may indicate that a portfolio is less diversified across holdings than the number of funds alone suggests.

However, overlap is not automatically bad. It should be considered alongside fund objectives, sector exposure, asset allocation, costs, risk, and investment goals.

## How to Check Overlap

A useful overlap analysis requires reasonably current portfolio data and a clear calculation method. Some tools count common holdings, while others calculate overlap using the portfolio weights of those holdings.

Results can change when the underlying funds rebalance their portfolios.

If a website offers a mutual fund overlap tool, it should explain the methodology, the date of the portfolio data, and any limitations.

## Are a Screener, Comparison Tool and Overlap Tool the Same?

No.

A screener filters a fund universe. A comparison tool places selected funds side by side. An overlap tool examines common holdings across portfolios.

These tools answer related but different questions. They should not be treated as a single investment recommendation system.

## Conclusion

Use a mutual fund screener to filter options, comparison tools to review relevant characteristics, and overlap analysis to understand common holdings. Interpret all results in context and verify data freshness.
