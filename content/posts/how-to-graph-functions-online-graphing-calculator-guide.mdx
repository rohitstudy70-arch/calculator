## Introduction to 2D Function Graphing

In mathematics, **graphing a function** provides an immediate geometric representation of an algebraic relationship. When you express an equation in the form:

$$y = f(x)$$

every real number input $x$ in the domain yields a corresponding real output $y$ in the range. Mapping these ordered coordinate pairs $(x, y)$ onto a **Cartesian coordinate plane** transforms equations into intuitive curves, illustrating continuity, symmetry, rates of growth, and turning points.

## The Anatomy of a 2D Cartesian Coordinate Graph

Understanding a graph requires familiarity with five foundational structural components:

1. **Horizontal X-Axis (Abscissa):** Represents the independent variable $x$. As you move left to right, $x$ increases.
2. **Vertical Y-Axis (Ordinate):** Represents the dependent variable $y = f(x)$. Moving upward indicates higher positive values.
3. **Origin $(0, 0)$:** The central intersection of both axes where $x = 0$ and $y = 0$.
4. **X-Intercepts (Roots or Zeros):** Any coordinate where the curve intersects the horizontal axis:
   $$f(x) = 0$$
5. **Y-Intercept:** The point where the curve crosses the vertical axis:
   $$(0, f(0))$$

## Key Families of Mathematical Curves

### 1. Linear Functions: $f(x) = mx + c$
- **Characteristics:** Straight line with constant slope $m$ and vertical intercept $c$.
- **Example:** $f(x) = 2x + 1$ has a slope of $2$ (rises $2$ units for every $1$ unit moved right) and crosses the y-axis at $(0, 1)$.

### 2. Quadratic Functions (Parabolas): $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$
- **Characteristics:** Symmetrical U-shaped curve with a single vertex.
- **Example:** $f(x) = x^2 - 4$ has a minimum turning point at $(0, -4)$ and real roots at $x = 2$ and $x = -2$.

### 3. Cubic Polynomials: $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$
- **Characteristics:** S-shaped curve displaying local maximums and minimums (turning points).
- **Behavior:** Crosses the axis at $x = 0$ and $x = \pm\sqrt{3} \approx \pm 1.73$.

### 4. Periodic Trigonometric Waves: $f(x) = \sin(x)$ & $f(x) = \cos(x)$
- **Characteristics:** Continuous wave oscillations with bounded range $[-1, 1]$ and period $2\pi \approx 6.283$.
- **Applications:** Signal processing, acoustics, physics harmonic motion, and alternating current circuits.

### 5. Rational Functions with Asymptotes: $f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$
- **Characteristics:** Hyperbolic branches in Quadrants I and III.
- **Asymptote:** As $x \to 0$, $y \to \pm\infty$. The y-axis ($x = 0$) serves as a vertical asymptote that the curve never touches.

## Step-by-Step Guide: How to Plot Any Function

Follow this structured workflow to plot any mathematical equation using an online plotter:

1. **Isolate the Dependent Variable:** Ensure the expression is in terms of $x$ (e.g. rewrite $2y - 4x = 6$ as $f(x) = 2x + 3$).
2. **Type the Expression into the Plotter:** Use standard mathematical operators:
   - Powers: `x^2`, `x^3`
   - Multiplication: `2*x` or implicit `2x`
   - Division: `1/(x+1)` (always use grouping parentheses)
   - Functions: `sin(x)`, `cos(x)`, `sqrt(x)`, `ln(x)`, `abs(x)`
3. **Select Viewing Window Limits:** Standard viewing intervals $[-10, 10]$ on both axes capture most school and college algebra problems.
4. **Locate Critical Features:** Identify the real roots marked on the axis, examine the vertex, and observe behavior as $x \to \infty$.

## Summary Table: Function Behaviors Compared

| Function Type | General Equation | Distinctive Shape | Number of Real Roots |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Linear** | $f(x) = mx + c$ | Straight Line | Exactly 1 (if $m \neq 0$) |
| **Quadratic** | $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ | Parabola | 0, 1, or 2 roots |
| **Cubic** | $f(x) = ax^3 + \dots$ | S-Curve | 1 to 3 roots |
| **Exponential** | $f(x) = a^x$ | J-Curve (Steep Growth) | 0 (if $a > 0$) |
| **Trigonometric** | $f(x) = \sin(x)$ | Harmonic Wave | Infinite periodic roots |
